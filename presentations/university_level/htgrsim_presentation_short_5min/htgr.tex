\begin{frame}{What is a High Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor (HTGR)?}

\begin{columns}

\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}

\begin{itemize}
\item Uses helium gas as coolant
\item Uses graphite to slow neutrons
\item Produces heat through nuclear fission
\item Converts heat into electricity
\item Designed with strong passive safety features
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{block}{Key Idea}
A nuclear reactor is a heat source.

The heat is used to generate electricity.
\end{block}

\begin{frame}{How Does Heat Become Electricity?}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The reactor produces heat.
\item Helium carries heat away from the reactor.
\item A steam generator transfers heat to water.
\item Steam drives a turbine.
\item The turbine generates electricity.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{block}{Energy Conversion Chain}
Reactor $\rightarrow$ Helium Coolant $\rightarrow$ Steam Generator
$\rightarrow$ Turbine $\rightarrow$ Electricity
\end{block}

\vspace{0.3cm}

Everything shown in the simulator is part of this process.

\end{frame}
