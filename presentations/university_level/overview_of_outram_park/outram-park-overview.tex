\section{The Shape of the Thing}
% =============================================================================

\begin{frame}{What OUTRAM PARK is}
    A multi-physics simulation workspace written in Rust, assembled mostly by
    porting established open-source engineering codes rather than inventing new
    ones.

    \medskip
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{38 crates}, \textbf{1.27 million lines} of Rust.
        \item \textbf{1{,}676 commits}, first commit 25 November 2024.
        \item \textbf{8{,}453} declared test functions across the workspace.
        \item \textbf{11 of 38} crates are declared \emph{mature}; the other 27
              explicitly are not.
    \end{itemize}

    \medskip
    The prior throughout is \textbf{``this probably exists already''}. Most
    modules are ports of OpenFOAM \parencite{weller1998tensorial},
    OpenMC \parencite{romano2015openmc}, NJOY2016
    \parencite{macfarlane2017njoy}, CoolProp \parencite{bell2014coolprop} and
    similar --- with the upstream kept as the thing to be measured against.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What ``mature'' is allowed to mean}
    Maturity here is a \emph{declared} status with a written bar, not a feeling
    about code quality. Each mature crate states its own acceptance bar and the
    class of evidence behind it:

    \medskip
    \statustab{
    \begin{tabular}{ll}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Evidence class} & \textbf{What it means} \\
        \midrule
        Reference standard & Reproduces a published standard to stated tolerance \\
        Cross-code         & Agrees with the upstream code it was ported from \\
        Experimental       & Reproduces a measured experiment \\
        Analytical / MMS   & Matches closed-form solutions; convergence order verified \\
        Unit + consistency & Limiting cases exact; published constants reproduced \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}

    \medskip
    A crate with no such written statement is \textbf{by definition not mature}.
    Only the maintainer may declare maturity.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reading the tables that follow}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{kLOC} --- thousands of lines of Rust in the crate,
              measured 2026-09-15.
        \item \textbf{Tests} --- \emph{declared} test functions counted
              statically (\texttt{\#[test]} attributes). This is an inventory
              count, not a record of a passing run.
        \item \textbf{M} --- \checkmark{} if declared mature on 2026-09-13.
    \end{itemize}

    \medskip
    \textbf{Measured on 2026-09-15:} \texttt{cargo check --workspace --lib
    --tests} completes clean. The three most recently merged crates were run in
    full: \textbf{1{,}111 tests passed, 0 failed}.

    \medskip
    Everything else below is inventory and declared status --- stated as such,
    because the difference matters.
\end{frame}

\section{Neutron Transport and Criticality}
% =============================================================================

\begin{frame}{Neutron transport: 97.8 kLOC, 603 declared tests}
    \statustab{
    \begin{tabular}{lrrc}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Crate} & \textbf{kLOC} & \textbf{Tests} & \textbf{M} \\
        \midrule
        \texttt{outram-mc-libs}         & 74.7 & 392 & \checkmark \\
        \texttt{boon-lay}               & 21.4 & 187 & \\
        \texttt{outram-park-fork-onix}  &  1.7 &  24 & \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}

    \medskip
    \texttt{outram-mc-libs} ports the OpenMC Monte Carlo transport kernels
    \parencite{romano2015openmc}. \texttt{boon-lay} carries Lagrangian
    transport and transmutation; \texttt{outram-park-fork-onix} is an early
    port of the ONIX depletion code \parencite{lanversin2021onix} and is barely
    started.

    \medskip
    \textbf{Bar (\texttt{outram-mc-libs}):} $k_{\text{eff}}$ within
    \textbf{500 pcm} of the ICSBEP Godiva benchmark
    \parencite{icsbep2021}.

    \medskip
    A separate gate covers the \textbf{HTR-10 double-heterogeneity difference}
    \parencite{iaea2003htr10} --- that difference alone, deliberately, and
    nothing else.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Neutron transport: what the last month actually did}
    The interesting work here was \emph{finding errors}, not adding features:

    \medskip
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Surface tracking computed nested-universe surface normals about
              the \textbf{wrong centre}. Fixing it moved LEU-COMP-THERM-008
              from $+2341$ pcm to $\mathbf{+37}$ \textbf{pcm} against a
              measured critical, and moved the hex lattice by 1769 pcm.
        \item The delta-tracking majorant \textbf{under-bounded} $\Sigma_t$
              below its grid floor by up to $9\times$ --- an outright
              correctness bug in the sampling.
        \item The ring-RPT pebble case now reproduces exact tracking to
              $+37$ pcm at $9\times$ statistics.
        \item A previously claimed throughput speed-up was \textbf{measured and
              refuted} (GH \#205); reproducibility was confirmed instead.
    \end{itemize}

    \medskip
    The 500 pcm bar is set at \emph{what the code achieves today}, not at what
    criticality safety work should eventually demand.
\end{frame}

        \texttt{outram-park-fork-dwsim-libs}    &  92.7 &  721 & \checkmark \\
        \texttt{outram-park-fork-coolprop}      &  69.4 &  363 & \\
        \texttt{outram-park-fork-thermochimica} &   1.7 &    6 & \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}

    \medskip
    The most heavily tested module in the workspace --- appropriately, since
    everything else calls into it.

    \medskip
    \textbf{Bar (steam tables):} IF97 region equations to $10^{-8}$ against
    published IAPWS values \parencite{wagner2000iapws}; flash tables held to a
    looser bar (0.5\% volume, 8\% thermal conductivity).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Properties: recent work, and one caveat that matters}
    \textbf{Steam tables.} The $(\rho,h)$ flash now works by inverting the IF97
    backward equations; $(p,h)$, $(p,s)$ and $(h,s)$ cover Region 5. Two
    \emph{negative} results were recorded on where $(\rho,h)$ does not belong,
    with conditioning measured on the answer rather than on a local slope.

    \medskip
    \textbf{DWSIM port --- read this before citing it.} The bar is agreement
    with upstream DWSIM \parencite{dwsim} to 4 significant figures. Upstream
    has since been built and run headless, and the Peng--Robinson $Z$ agrees to
    \textbf{6 significant figures}.

    \medskip
    But that is \textbf{the EOS layer only}. The flash-layer comparison the bar
    also names is still open, and \textbf{0 of the 107 rows} in its
    port-coverage matrix are marked \textsc{ported + validated}.

    \medskip
    \texttt{outram-park-fork-thermochimica} \parencite{piro2013thermochimica}
    is a stub: 1.7 kLOC, 6 tests.
\end{frame}

        \midrule
        \texttt{outram-park-fork-offbeat} & 61.3 & 550 & \\
        \texttt{farrer-park}              & 16.1 &  99 & \checkmark \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}

    \medskip
    \texttt{outram-park-fork-offbeat} ports the OFFBEAT fuel behaviour solver
    \parencite{scolaro2020offbeat} --- substantial at 61 kLOC, but
    \textbf{not declared mature}. \texttt{farrer-park} is the finite-element
    layer underneath it.

    \medskip
    \textbf{Bar (\texttt{farrer-park}):} MMS $L_2$ convergence order within
    0.15 of theory per element; patch test to $10^{-12}$; Lam\'e
    \emph{displacement} within 1\%, \emph{stress} on observed order.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{\texttt{farrer-park}: mature, with a known uncured defect}
    This crate is the clearest illustration of what the maturity label does and
    does not promise.

    \medskip
    \begin{itemize}
        \item It carries a \textbf{known uncured defect}: shear locking,
              measured at \textbf{66.7\% too stiff} on Quad4 elements at
              element aspect ratio 4.
        \item Its maturity therefore says \emph{the numerics it implements
              converge at the theoretical rate} --- \textbf{not} that every
              element is fit for bending.
        \item It is the only roster entry declared \textbf{within days of the
              crate first existing}, on an analytical/MMS bar with
              \textbf{no cross-code and no benchmark leg at all}.
    \end{itemize}

    \medskip
    Declared mature 2026-09-13 by maintainer decision, with all of the above
    written into the declaration rather than omitted from it.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What is explicitly not validated}
    Stated plainly, because the roster states it plainly:

    \medskip
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{DWSIM port} --- EOS layer only. 0 of 107 port-coverage
              rows are \textsc{ported + validated}. Flash layer open.
        \item \textbf{Granular DEM} --- integrator and contact laws validated;
              \textbf{granular physics is not}.
        \item \textbf{\texttt{farrer-park}} --- shear locking uncured, 66.7\%
              too stiff on Quad4 at aspect ratio 4; no benchmark leg.
        \item \textbf{\texttt{teh-o-prke}} --- no analytical transient
              validation.
        \item \textbf{\texttt{outram-mc-libs}} --- 500 pcm is today's
              achievement, not a criticality-safety standard.
        \item \textbf{27 of 38 crates} carry no maturity declaration at all,
              including 61 kLOC of fuel performance and 69 kLOC of CoolProp.
        \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Where the work is going}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Close the DWSIM flash layer.} The EOS leg is done to 6
              significant figures; the 107-row matrix is the remaining work,
              and it is the clearest single gap on the roster.
        \item \textbf{Give \texttt{teh-o-prke} an analytical transient leg},
              and \texttt{farrer-park} a cross-code or benchmark leg.
        \item \textbf{Cure shear locking} --- or document the element as
              unfit for bending and select around it.
        \item \textbf{Tighten the Monte Carlo bar} below 500 pcm now that the
              nested-universe and majorant defects are fixed.
        \item \textbf{Declare or retire} the large undeclared ports:
              OFFBEAT, CoolProp, PFLOTRAN, the twin engine.
    \end{itemize}

    \medskip
    The discipline that produced the last month's results --- measuring claims
    and \emph{retracting} the ones that fail --- is the part worth keeping.
\end{frame}
